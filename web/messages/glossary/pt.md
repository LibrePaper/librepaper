# Brazilian Portuguese glossary (pt)

## 1. Register and conventions

- Address: "você" (informal-neutral, standard in Brazilian software). Use third-person verb forms. Avoid "tu" and "o senhor". Avoid the pronoun when the sentence reads well without it.
- Buttons and menu items: infinitive ("Salvar", "Exportar", "Compartilhar"). Short labels may be nouns ("Histórico", "Configurações"). Imperative is allowed only in instructions inside sentences ("Digite seu identificador").
- Vocabulary is pt-BR: arquivo, tela, salvar, baixar, usuário, compartilhar, lixeira, aba, mouse, gerenciar. Never European forms (ficheiro, ecrã, guardar, utilizador, partilhar, separador, rato).
- Spelling follows the 1990 Orthographic Agreement (ideia, não "idéia"; sem trema in "frequência").
- Quotation marks: curly double quotes “ ” as in the English source; inside them use ‘ ’. Do not use « ».
- Ellipsis: the single character … with no space before it ("Salvando…").
- Colon, semicolon, question and exclamation marks: no space before them.
- Decimal separator is a comma, thousands separator is a period or a non-breaking space ("1.234", "3,5 MB"). Use the placeholders as given; do not reformat numbers that come from the code.
- Percent: "50%" with no space (common in pt-BR software).
- No spaces around "/" in "e/ou".
- Gender-neutral wording where it costs nothing ("pessoa", "quem edita"). Otherwise use the masculine generic for roles (proprietário, editor, leitor), as mainstream software does.
- Keep placeholders such as {count} and {path} untouched. Plural forms: pt has "one" and "other" (0 and 1 are "one" in pt-BR; check the catalogue's plural rules).
- No em dashes or en dashes in translations. Use commas, colons or parentheses.

## 5. Kept in English

- Product, format and tool names: LibrePaper, LaTeX, Typst, Quarto, Markdown, HTML, PDF, DOCX, Word, Zotero, GitHub, Git, Harper, Pandoc, BibTeX, Biber, TeX Live, Vim, Emacs, Claude Code, Codex, Claude, Loro.
- Keyboard key names stay English-style as on Brazilian keyboards: Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Enter, Esc, Tab. "Backspace" stays. Arrow keys may use symbols.
- Git words kept: commit, push, pull, branch, fork (Git sense in prose), token, chat, link, backup, status, e-mail written as "e-mail".
- Words people use as English loans in Brazil: offline, online, download (noun), login (noun in prose), app (avoid where "aplicativo" fits).
- Code, file extensions, commands and URLs are never translated.

## 6. Units and numbers

- Byte units: B, KB, MB, GB, TB, written with a space before the unit ("12 MB"). Do not translate to "bytes" abbreviations like "o". The word "bytes" is used in running text ("{bytes} bytes").
- Decimal comma ("1,5 GB"). Thousands separator is a period or a non-breaking space when the code does not format the number.
- Counts: "{count} arquivo" / "{count} arquivos"; "{count} palavra" / "{count} palavras". Zero and one take the singular in pt-BR.
- Dates and times: day/month/year (31/10/2026), 24-hour clock. Rely on the locale-aware formatter in the code.
- Percent: "50%" without a space.
