\chapter{Methods}\label{chap:methods}

This chapter describes the estimators and the assumptions behind them. The notation stays the same throughout the book.

\section{Estimators}\label{sec:estimators}

The bootstrap estimates the sampling distribution of a statistic by resampling the data with replacement \citep{efron1979}. For a statistic $\hat\theta$ computed on $B$ resamples, the bootstrap estimate of its standard error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bootstrap-se}
  \widehat{\operatorname{se}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{B-1} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \bigl(\hat\theta^{*}_b - \bar\theta^{*}\bigr)^2},
  \qquad
  \bar\theta^{*} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \hat\theta^{*}_b.
\end{equation}
\cref{eq:bootstrap-se} needs no distributional assumption, which is why it is the default check in this book.

\section{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}

Each estimator relies on the same design. Units are randomized to treatment, and the outcome follows the linear model introduced in \cref{sec:model-notation}. Violations of randomization are outside the scope of this chapter, and \cref{chap:introduction} points to the literature that covers them.

\section{Model notation}\label{sec:model-notation}

Let $Y_i$ be the outcome and $D_i \in \{0,1\}$ the treatment for unit $i$. The statistic of interest is the difference in means between the two arms, which estimates the treatment effect $\beta_1$.
