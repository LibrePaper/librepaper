Any format

Markdown, HTML, Typst, LaTeX and Quarto all publish to the same reader. The compiler runs in the reader’s own browser, so somebody you send a link to needs no TeX installation, no toolchain, and nothing but the page.

Collaborate

Several people read, annotate and edit at once. Two in the same sentence converge without either waiting; each keeps their own undo history and sees the others’ carets by name. Threads hang off a passage, or off a region of a figure.

A link carries its own role — reader, commenter, editor or owner — so what you send decides what they can do.

History & track changes

Replying to a comment is writing the response to reviewers. Export a round of review and each remark comes back with the passage as that reviewer saw it, what became of it since, and your reply underneath.

Under it: checkpoints you can name, a word diff between any two, and revisions to accept or reject one at a time.

LLM agents

Bring your own agent. No additional subscription.

Three skills ship inside the binary, and an agent works through a link with exactly that link’s permissions — a read link reads, a comment link annotates. Your model credentials stay on your machine; the document server never sees them and runs no inference.

Writing tools

BibTeX and BibLaTeX bibliographies with real citation styles, mathematics, and papers that are a directory rather than a file — the chapters, the figures and the .bib go up together and stay together.

Self-host

One static binary, with the reader, the renderers and the server compiled into it. On your laptop, or on a small machine that can keep a process running.

You decide who may publish and who may comment, how long a document lives, and where the LaTeX compiler is fetched from. Nothing you publish leaves the machine you run it on. 

librepaper admin serve

Free and open source

Nothing on this page is gated. Every feature is available to everyone, for nothing, right now — no paid tier, no trial, no seat count, and nothing held back for a version that costs money.

The one limit is storage: 100 MB per publisher. We may offer very cheap storage accounts later for people who need more room. Features will not move behind them.

The whole of it — the server, the reader and the renderers — is open and developed in the open, and what you put in comes back out: annotations export as Markdown, or as W3C Web Annotation JSON-LD that another tool can read. A review made here is not stranded here.
